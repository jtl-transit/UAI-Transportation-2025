# UAI 2025 Transportation: Recitations

In this folder, you will find all the recitation material for the four _UAI: Transportation_ lectures. 

## Recitation 1: Urban Mobility and AI
This foundational recitation introduces students to AI applications in transportation through Professor Zhao's framework of defining success and the future. Students explore how technological advancement doesn't automatically equal societal benefit, citing Beijing's transformation from a sustainable, electrified, active-mode city to a car-dominated, congested one as a key example. The recitation guides students through four exercises: Defining success metrics for their chosen domain beyond economic indicators, creating analytics frameworks that connect supply and demand through AI-enabled planning and operations, conceptualizing grounded AI applications that are problem-driven and human-centered, and developing communication strategies for diverse stakeholders. Students learn to bridge behavioral thinking (travel as social and emotional) with computational thinking (data and algorithms) to create responsible AI systems that address safety, sustainability, equity, and efficiency challenges in transportation and their own fields of interest.

## Recitation 2: Discrete Choice Modeling
This advanced recitation explores the integration of discrete choice models with deep neural networks using a Train vs Car Choice Dataset from The Netherlands. Students implement and compare three modeling approaches: classical Logistic Regression and Multinomial Logit (MNL) for economic interpretability, Fully Connected Deep Neural Networks (FC-DNN) for "black box" predictive power, and theory-informed Alternative-Specific Utility Deep Neural Networks (ASU-DNN) that respect utility maximization theory. Through hands-on implementation, students learn to prepare choice data, extract choice probabilities, and understand the trade-offs between interpretability and predictive performance. The recitation emphasizes how theory-informed neural architectures can combine the flexibility of deep learning with the economic foundations of discrete choice theory.

## Recitation 3: Multimodal Machine Learning for Transit Data
This practical recitation demonstrates real-world AI applications in public transportation through case studies from MIT's collaborations with major transit authorities (TfL, CTA, Hong Kong MTR, MBTA, Singapore LTA, WMATA). Students implement two core methodologies: transformer-based sentiment analysis using MetRoBERTa to convert unstructured social media messages into structured operational insights, and computer vision-based crowding detection using semantic segmentation to estimate passenger density from CCTV footage. Students are exposed to different modes of transit data, address computational trade-offs between accuracy and efficiency in CCTV sampling.

## Recitation 4: Generative Urban AI
This recitation explores generative AI applications in transportation through the emerging "create" function that enables AI to generate novel solutions rather than just predict or analyze. Students work with conditional diffusion models (ControlNet + Stable Diffusion) to implement an urban planning systems that respect spatial constraints and design objectives. The hands-on implementation demonstrates how planners can specify land-use proportions, preserve existing infrastructure from OpenStreetMap data, and apply different urban textures while maintaining design constraints through natural language prompting. The recitation also asks the students to designing comprehensive generative AI systems for a chosen domains, emphasizing how the "create" function can transform traditional practices from months-long iterative processes to immediate, interactive engagement while maintaining professional expertise and domain validity.
